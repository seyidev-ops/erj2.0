Four days a week. Two young children. A full-time job in Abuja. No weekends available, no late nights, everything async.

Six hours a week. Fifty-four hours across nine weeks — less than a week and a half of full-time work.

Her income before: ₦2.1M a year. About $1,380 in dollar terms, and falling every time the naira moved.

Her offer nine weeks later: $38,400 a year, paid through Deel by a US supply-chain SaaS company. 27.8× in dollar terms.

Here is the part I want you to sit with. She had the same skills in week 9 that she had in week 1. Nothing was added to her ability.

What changed was three things:

Representation — the same experience rewritten in the language global employers actually read. Two wins she had never written down anywhere became the spine of her CV: a 34% reduction in order errors, an 11% saving in delivery costs. Both already achieved. Both invisible until they were written.

Evidence — those wins turned into a three-screen portfolio. A one-day build. Claims became exhibits.

Direction — fourteen personalised messages to named humans at companies with verified global hiring infrastructure. Six replied. Three became interview processes. One became an offer.

For contrast, another documented journey on our site: 312 tracked applications over eleven months, 308 silences, zero offers. Same country, same market, same year.

Fourteen beat three hundred and twelve because the fourteen were aimed.

The annual difference between her before and after is roughly $37,000, produced by 54 hours of correctly aimed work. That is about $685 an hour, counted against year one alone.

Her constraints were real. They did not disqualify her. They only decided the pace.
