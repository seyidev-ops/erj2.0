Ghosted After the Interview: What to Send, and When to Stop

Two weeks of silence after a call that went well. Here is what is usually happening, the two messages worth sending, and when to close the file.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 15 June 2026 4 min read

It went well. They said they would come back to you this week. That was eighteen days ago.

What is usually actually happening

In order of likelihood: the role was paused or the budget moved; the hiring manager went on leave; they are still interviewing and do not want to lose you while they decide; an internal candidate appeared; or the person handling it simply forgot in a week that got away from them.

Notice how few of these are about you. Silence feels personal and almost never is — which matters, because the story you tell yourself here determines whether you keep applying next week.

The two messages

Message one, at seven days past the date they promised. Same thread, short, no reproach.

"Hi [Name], just floating this back up — you mentioned coming back to me around [date]. No pressure at all if the timeline has shifted; happy to wait. Still very interested."

Message two, ten days after that. And this is the last one.

"Hi [Name], I will stop chasing after this one. If the role has gone another way, no hard feelings at all — I would just appreciate knowing so I can close it off. And if it is still live, I am still interested."

That second message has a noticeably higher reply rate than the first, because it releases the pressure and gives them an easy, guilt-free way to answer.

Then stop

Two messages. After that, mark it closed in your tracker and put the energy into this week's list. A third follow-up will not change the outcome and it makes the eventual reconnection awkward.

The mistake here is not the ghosting — it is spending three weeks emotionally parked on one role while sending nothing new. Your pipeline is what protects you from this, which is why the weekly rhythm matters more than any single application.

If they do reject you, ask

"Thanks for letting me know — I appreciate you closing the loop. If you have thirty seconds: was there one thing that would have made me a stronger candidate? I am genuinely trying to improve rather than argue the decision."

Roughly one in four replies, and that one reply is often worth more than the interview was. Ask every time. Never argue with the answer.

The reframe

A ghosted process is not a verdict. It is one company's internal mess landing on you. Keep the file, keep the contact, and if a similar role opens there in six months, message the same person. People remember candidates who handled silence gracefully.
