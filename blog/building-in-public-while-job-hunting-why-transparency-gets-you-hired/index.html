Building in Public While Job Hunting: Why Transparency Gets You Hired Faster

Sharing your job search progress publicly on LinkedIn is counterintuitive and one of the most effective ways to attract inbound opportunities. Here is the evidence.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 7 June 2026 5 min read

The Counterintuitive Truth

Most job seekers hide their search. They apply quietly, interview silently, and announce a new role only after starting. This misses one of the most effective free tools available: public accountability and deliberate visibility.

What Building in Public Looks Like in a Job Search

  • A weekly LinkedIn post: "Week 3 of my remote job search: sent 15 direct messages, got 4 replies, booked 2 calls. Here is what I learned this week."
  • A lesson post after each interview: "Had my first remote interview. One question I was not ready for — and how I will answer it next time."
  • A process post: "I have been using Notion to track every application. Here is exactly what I track and why."

Why It Works

Your ideal hiring manager is scrolling LinkedIn. They see someone thoughtful, who learns visibly, communicates clearly, and shows real growth over time. That person is already demonstrating the qualities of a strong remote worker before the interview even begins.

Three Cohort 6 participants who started posting weekly job search updates received inbound messages from hiring managers within two weeks. Not from applications — from posts.

What Not to Do

Do not post frustration without a lesson. Do not name companies that rejected you. Do not make it about what you want — make it about what you are learning. Audiences follow growth, not complaints.
