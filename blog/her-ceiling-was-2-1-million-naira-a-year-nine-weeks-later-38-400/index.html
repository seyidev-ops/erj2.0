Her Ceiling Was 2.1 Million Naira a Year. Nine Weeks Later: $38,400.

Amara had 90 minutes a day, four days a week, two young children and a full-time job. No international CV, no portfolio, no LinkedIn, no contacts abroad. Fifty-four hours of work later she signed at $38,400 — 27.8x her old income in dollar terms. Here is the entire ledger.

Oluwaseyi Ashiru 29 July 2026 8 min read

This is one documented journey, published with the numbers attached — the before, the after, the hours, and the honest arithmetic in between. It is not a promise of a typical result. It is a demonstration of what the ceiling actually is: not talent, not luck, and not the number of hours you have.

The before: a hard naira ceiling

Amara, 35, Operations Manager in Abuja. Competent, promoted, respected. Her income had reached the top of what her role could pay and then stopped moving.

  • Income: ₦2.1M per year — about ₦175,000 a month
  • In dollar terms: roughly $1,380 a year, and falling every time the naira moved
  • International CV: none
  • Portfolio: none
  • LinkedIn: none
  • Contacts abroad: none

This is the part most people misread. The ceiling was not her ability. Her ability was already worth several times what she was being paid. The ceiling was the currency she was being paid in, and the fact that nobody outside her city could see her.

The constraint: 90 minutes, four days a week

Two young children. A demanding day job. No weekends available, no late nights, everything async. When we started, the honest budget was ninety minutes a day, four days a week.

Write that out and it looks like a reason to postpone: 6 hours a week. 54 hours across nine weeks. Less than a week and a half of full-time work, spread across two months.

Most people do not fail for lack of hours. They fail because every hour they do have is aimed in a direction that cannot produce the outcome.

The nine weeks: what those 54 hours were spent on

Weeks 1–3 — Representation

Her CV was rebuilt around metrics rather than duties. Two real wins she had never written down anywhere became the spine of it: a 34% reduction in order errors and an 11% saving in delivery costs. Both true, both already achieved, both invisible until they were written in the language a global hiring manager reads.

Weeks 3–5 — Evidence

Those same two wins became a Notion portfolio — problem, action, number, in three screens. Not a design project. A one-day build that turned claims into exhibits.

Weeks 5–6 — Visibility

LinkedIn built from a blank page, written for a remote-first supply-chain audience rather than for her current employer.

Weeks 6–9 — Outreach

Fourteen personalised messages. Not applications — messages, to named humans at companies with verified global hiring infrastructure.

The outreach ledger

  • 14 personalised outreach messages sent
  • 6 replies — a 43% reply rate
  • 3 interview processes entered
  • 1 offer signed, from a US supply-chain SaaS company, paid through Deel

Compare that with another documented journey on our success stories page: 312 tracked applications, 308 silences, zero offers over eleven months. Same country, same market, same year. Fourteen messages beat three hundred and twelve applications because the fourteen were aimed and the three hundred and twelve were sprayed.

The after: a dollar reality

  • Offer: $38,400 per year — $3,200 a month, paid in dollars via Deel
  • Multiple: 27.8x her previous income in dollar terms
  • Hours: 7am–3pm WAT, async-first, no timezone penalty, still in Abuja
  • Skills on day one of week 9: the same skills she had on day one of week 1

The arithmetic nobody runs

The annual difference between the before and after is roughly $37,000. It was produced by 54 hours of correctly aimed work.

That is about $685 for every hour she spent, counted against the first year alone. Year two pays it again. Year three pays it again. No investment she could have made with ₦175,000 a month comes within a hundred miles of that number.

Her constraints were real. Two children, a full job, ninety minutes, four days. The constraints did not disqualify her. They only decided the pace.

What actually changed

Not her talent. Not her work ethic — she had always had that; it was being spent locally at local rates. Three things changed:

  1. Representation — the same experience, written in the language global employers actually read
  2. Evidence — claims converted into exhibits a stranger can verify in ninety seconds
  3. Direction — fourteen aimed messages instead of hundreds of applications into systems that were never going to reply

That is the whole system. It is not mysterious, it is not fast, and it is entirely learnable inside ninety minutes a day.

The honest part

Not everyone lands at $38,400. Some land at $500 a month and climb. Some take five months rather than nine weeks. Some need to build a skill first, and no representation work can substitute for that — which is exactly why our register page asks whether you have a sellable skill before it asks about your budget.

What is consistent across every documented journey is narrower and more useful than a salary figure: the naira ceiling is a positioning problem far more often than it is a competence problem. People are not underpaid because they are not good. They are underpaid because they are invisible to the only buyers who pay in dollars.
